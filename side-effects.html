Mitocore Side Effects: What to Know

A plain-language overview of reported reactions, contraindications, and who should be cautious with Ortho Molecular Mitocore.

The short version of side effects: most people are fine. The most common issues are stomach upset (eat first), feeling wired (you may be B-vitamin sensitive — start lower), and trouble sleeping (don't take it after lunch).

Most Commonly Reported Reactions

Across user reports and practitioner observation, the side effects most often associated with Mitocore fall into a few categories:

  • Upset stomach — take it with food, every time
  • Feeling wired — start with one capsule, work up if you're sensitive
  • Headaches early on — usually a week or so, then they ease up
  • Trouble sleeping — don't take it after lunch
  • Nothing noticeable — give it six weeks before you decide it isn't working

Who Should Be Cautious

Things to watch for: blood thinners, certain heart medications (nitroglycerin), diabetes medications, methotrexate, pregnancy, and chemotherapy. Bring the bottle to your next appointment if any of those apply.

What to Do If You Experience a Reaction

If a reaction occurs, the standard guidance is to stop the supplement and contact your healthcare provider. A clinician can review the full ingredient list, your other medications and supplements, and any underlying conditions that may be relevant. For a deeper look at how a practitioner evaluates Mitocore side effects in real patients, see this the practitioner write-up linked here.

Drug and Supplement Interactions

The real interactions: warfarin, methotrexate, nitroglycerin, diabetes medications (insulin and the oral ones), and chemotherapy drugs. None of them are absolutes — they're conversations to have with whoever wrote the prescription.

Long-Term Use Considerations

Most people who use it use it for several months at a time. If you've been on it for a year without a check-in, that's a good time to talk to your provider about whether you still need it.

Bottom line. Mitocore is generally well-tolerated and worth a four-to-six-week trial if your practitioner suggested it for fatigue or brain fog. If you're on prescription medications, loop in your prescriber first. If nothing changes in eight weeks, it probably isn't the right tool for your specific picture.
